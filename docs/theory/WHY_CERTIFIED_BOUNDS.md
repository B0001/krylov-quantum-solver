# Why Certified Bounds

*Companion to `complexity-proof-graph-v2.cypher`. Citations arXiv-verified 2026-07-16.*

This solver refuses to report a ground-state energy it cannot bound from both sides. That is not conservatism for its own sake; it is the only posture consistent with what is actually proven about this problem.

The problem this solver operates on — ground-state energy estimation of the electronic Hamiltonian restricted to a fixed orbital basis and fixed electron number — is QMA-complete (O'Gorman, Irani, Whitfield, Fefferman, arXiv:2103.08215, PRX Quantum 3, 020322). Not a simplified cousin of our problem: the problem itself, in exactly the regime where FCI and Krylov methods operate. The hardness construction is striking in what it does *not* need — the external potential is set to zero, and the hard instance is encoded entirely in the choice of Gaussian-superposition orbitals. The reduction chain runs from arbitrary QMA verification circuits through the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model (Piddock–Montanaro, arXiv:1506.04014) and the Fermi–Hubbard model with uniform repulsion to our exact problem statement. Even the classically-natural relaxation — finding the best single Slater determinant, i.e. Hartree–Fock — is NP-complete for this Hamiltonian class (same paper, Thm 2).

At the same time, none of this proves that any physically occurring molecule is a hard instance. The hardness is worst-case, over engineered bases the authors themselves call artificial. The complexity of the truly molecular problem — Coulomb Hamiltonian, potential arising solely from fixed point nuclei — is explicitly open (posed as such in arXiv:2103.08215; a fresh literature sweep on 2026-07-16 found no resolution). And the empirical frontier keeps pushing the other way: the FeMoco model, long the canonical quantum-advantage target, was solved classically to chemical accuracy in January 2026 (arXiv:2601.04621), and Fe₄S₄ CAS(54,36) benchmarks followed on GPU-accelerated DMRG (arXiv:2603.28648), following the broader skeptical assessment of exponential quantum advantage in ground-state chemistry (Lee et al., arXiv:2208.02199, Nat. Commun. 2023).

So the honest epistemic situation is: worst-case intractable in our precise regime; instance hardness unproven in either direction; heuristics — classical and quantum — routinely succeeding without guarantees. A solver that returns an unaccompanied number in this landscape is asking to be trusted on grounds that provably cannot exist in general. This solver instead returns an interval: a Rayleigh–Ritz upper bound that is unconditional, and a Temple/Lehmann-type lower bound that is explicit about its one conditional input (a spectral gap estimate). When the interval closes, the answer is certified regardless of whether the instance was hard. When it does not close, the solver says so loudly. That is the design principle the knowledge graph encodes as its terminal node, and the history of this project — a predecessor that silently returned impossible energies — is why it is non-bypassable.
